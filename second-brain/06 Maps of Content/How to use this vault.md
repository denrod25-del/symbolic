# How to use this vault

A **second brain** is an external, trusted store for the things you'd otherwise try (and fail) to keep in your head. The goal isn't to hoard notes — it's to make your best thinking *retrievable* and *reusable*.

## The method: CODE
1. **Capture** — keep what resonates. Don't judge in the moment; just save it to `00 Inbox` or your daily note.
2. **Organize** — sort by *actionability* using PARA, not by topic. Ask: "Which project/area does this serve?"
3. **Distill** — when you revisit a note, pull out the key idea in your own words (progressive summarization).
4. **Express** — the payoff: combine notes into something new — writing, a decision, a project.

## PARA in one line each
- **Projects** — has a goal *and* a deadline. Finite. → `01 Projects`
- **Areas** — a standard to hold indefinitely (Health, Finances, Career). → `02 Areas`
- **Resources** — a topic you're interested in. Reference material. → `03 Resources`
- **Archive** — anything from the above three that's now inactive. → `04 Archive`

> [!tip] The key insight
> The same note can belong to different folders at different times. Move things freely — PARA is about *what you're working on now*, not permanent classification.

## Note types (Zettelkasten-flavored)
- **Fleeting** — quick capture in the Inbox/daily note. Temporary.
- **Literature** — someone else's idea, in your words, with a link to the source.
- **Permanent** — one fully-formed idea of your own, linked into the web of notes.
- **MOC (Map of Content)** — a hand-curated hub linking related notes.

## Daily & weekly rhythm
- **Daily:** capture freely; at day's end, glance at the Inbox.
- **Weekly review:** empty the Inbox, update Project next-actions, archive what's finished, follow a few links and see what connects.

## Linking, the one rule that makes it a *brain*
Every note should link to at least one other note. Links are what turn a folder of files into a network you can navigate and that surfaces surprising connections in the Graph view (`Ctrl/Cmd + G`).
