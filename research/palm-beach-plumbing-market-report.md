# Palm Beach County Plumbing Market — Competitive Intelligence Report

_Prepared for a tech / water-quality-focused plumbing startup. June 2026._

**How to read this:** Every figure is sourced. Where review counts, employee counts, or
contaminant levels could not be independently confirmed (many review sites and utility PDFs
block automated access), they are flagged _unverified_ or _reported_ rather than presented as
fact. Three phases of the original playbook — phone secret-shopping, truck-wrap photography,
and private Facebook/Nextdoor groups — cannot be done from a desk and are listed as field work
at the end.

---

## Executive summary

1. **The market is crowded with plumbing, but thin on intelligence.** Dozens of competent
   full-service plumbers serve the county; almost none publish educational content,
   water-quality reports, video, or a customer portal. That void is real — and it is exactly
   where the "What's In Your Water" brand lives.
2. **The data backs the water angle with hard local facts**, not marketing fluff: county water
   is very hard (~19 gpg in PBCWUD areas), disinfected with **chloramine** (which defeats cheap
   big-box filters), and several systems have **PFOS/PFOA above the EPA's new 4-ppt limit** —
   with no mandated utility fix until **2031**.
3. **Communication, not price, is the #1 driver of dissatisfaction** (~38% vs ~21% in the best
   survey data) — yet written reviews are dominated by _billing surprises and upselling_. "The
   communication company" and "the no-upsell company" are both open positioning lanes.
4. **Of the four product ideas, only the Plumbing Health Score and the integrated bundle are
   genuine white space.** Water reports, AI home records, and predictive maintenance are already
   occupied by free tools or Fortune-500 hardware/insurance money. Don't out-build them — wrap
   them.

## Phase 2 — What the bad reviews say

The best survey evidence and the actual written 1-star reviews point in _slightly different
directions_ — and both are useful.

**The ranked pattern:**

1. **Poor communication** — survey #1 root cause. FIELDBOSS 2025 (1,000 homeowners): **38% cite
   poor communication/scheduling vs 21% higher-than-expected cost** — communication beats price
   ~2:1. Housecall Pro: ~1 in 3 list late/uncertain arrivals as top frustration; **46% say "On
   My Way" texts give the most reassurance.**
2. **Overpriced / billing surprises** — the most _vivid_ theme in written reviews.
3. **Late / no-show / scheduling.**
4. **High-pressure, fear-based upselling** ("your water heater is about to explode").
5. **Misdiagnosis / unnecessary work.**
6. **Dirty/sloppy/incomplete work.**
7. **Warranty problems** (stops responding on claims).

**Real local examples (illustrative, small sample):**

- **Flamingo Plumbing & Backflow (WPB):** BBB complaint — tech said a backflow device failed and
  quoted **$2,700 to replace it; a second company tested it and it passed.**
- **Hi-Tech (WPB):** BBB complaint — charged **$351 for augering the customer said not to do**;
  tech "wouldn't leave until payment was made."
- **Roto-Rooter (WPB):** "wanted **$14,000 for a repipe** while another company charged
  **$9,800**."
- **Benjamin Franklin (WPB):** "$375 for roof drain cleaning" and the drains "worsened."

**The actionable read:** The structural complaint (communication/reliability) and the emotional
complaint (price/upsell) are _both_ unguarded. "The Communication Plumbing Company" with text
updates, arrival tracking, photo/video summaries is validated by the survey data. A parallel
"flat, honest, no-upsell pricing" promise attacks the most emotionally charged reviews.
**Caveat:** there is no PBC-specific, plumbing-only percentage breakdown; the 38%/21% figures are
national home-services data. Avoid the "54% communication / 42% no-show" numbers online — they
trace to an uncited aggregator.

## Phase 6 — Hiring signals & licensing

**The labor market is tight and growing** — good for demand, hard for staffing:

- 130+ plumbing openings countywide on Indeed; aggressive **sign-on bonuses up to $3,000**,
  take-home trucks, gas cards, 401(k) match.
- **Pay (all sourced):** WPB plumber average **~$29.40/hr**, posted ranges **$23–$52/hr**;
  apprentices **$15–$38/hr**; a specific posting offered **$35→$45/hr after 90 days**; top
  earners **$2,500+/week** / $60k–$100k+ total comp. FL journeyman ~$31.68/hr; master ~$80k/yr.
- **Hard-to-fill:** experienced licensed/journeyman service techs.

**Florida licensing:**

- Governed by **DBPR / CILB**. **Certified (statewide)** vs **Registered (local-only)** is the
  key split.
- ~**4 years / ~8,000 hours** experience (incl. 1 yr supervisory); up to 3 yrs education can
  substitute.
- Two exams (**Trade Knowledge + Business & Finance**) via PSI/Prometric, ~70%+ to pass.
- Journeyman/master tiers are administered locally; the _statewide_ license is the
  Certified/Registered Plumbing Contractor. (DBPR checklist **CILB 5-M** is authoritative.)

**Implication:** The scaling constraint won't be demand — it will be hiring licensed techs. The
tech/brand story is also a _recruiting_ asset in a market where everyone fights for the same
journeymen.

## Local context — Palm Beach County water (the strongest weapon)

The county is **6+ separate utilities** with different source water, so the right answer
genuinely depends on the customer's address — which is the whole point of "intelligence."

- **Hardness:** Very hard in PBCWUD areas (**~19 gpg / ~317 ppm**); softer where utilities use
  nanofiltration (Boca, Seacoast report ~4–11 gpg). Scale on heaters/fixtures is a universal,
  visible pain.
- **Disinfectant: chloramine** (both WPB and PBCWUD), with an **annual summer free-chlorine
  flush** (e.g., WPB July 2025) that residents notice as taste/odor. Chloramine **defeats cheap
  big-box "chlorine" filters** — it needs catalytic carbon. A genuine expertise differentiator.
- **PFAS:** Reported **PFOS 18 ppt / PFOA 6.1 ppt** at PBCWUD points of entry (Nov 2024
  sampling); **Boynton ~26 ppt PFOS**; Delray historically much higher (PEER). EPA finalized
  **4-ppt MCLs for PFOA/PFOS in April 2024**, kept them in May 2025 but **extended compliance to
  2031**. Contamination is documented now; no mandated utility fix for years.
- **WPB is rare surface water** (Grassy Waters Preserve) — more organics, color, seasonal
  taste/odor — vs. aquifer wells everywhere else.

**Compliance caution for marketing:** All utilities currently meet _enforceable_ federal
standards; PFAS exceedances are against the _new, not-yet-enforced_ limits and some figures come
from advocacy groups. Say "above EPA's new health-based limits," **never** "illegal" or "unsafe."

## Phase 7 & 10 — Market-gap map (the skeptic's view)

Ranked from most-saturated (avoid head-on) to genuine white space:

| Customer problem | Who already owns it | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| "What's in my tap water?" (ZIP report) | EWG, HomeWater, Culligan, Leaf — **free incumbents** | **LOW** — lead magnet only |
| "Detect/stop a leak automatically" | Moen Flo, Phyn + insurers | **LOW** |
| "Predict equipment failure" | Moen/Phyn AI, insurer mandates | **LOW–MED** (service layer only) |
| "Store my home's repair/warranty records" | HomeBinder, HomeZada, ServiceTitan, C.L.U.E. (**Centriq died Jan 2026**) | **MED** — only the contractor-pushed, AI-populated version survives |
| "Is my pipe/corrosion risk high?" (consumer-facing) | Engineering calculators only | **MED (thin)** |
| **"One trusted, standardized Plumbing Health Score I track yearly"** | Ad-hoc 50-point checklists; no standard brand | **HIGH** |
| **"One plumber bundling water + score + records + monitoring"** | No one bundles all four | **HIGH** |

**The honest conclusion:** The "Water Intelligence Report" idea is _commoditized_ — EWG gives it
away free. Predictive maintenance is owned by Moen (Fortune Brands) and Phyn (Belkin/Uponor).
AI home-records is a graveyard (Centriq just shut down). **Don't invent these categories — wrap
them.** The two real white spaces are the **standardized Plumbing Health Score** (most defensible
as a B2B2C standard, since a single plumber self-scoring has an obvious conflict of interest) and
the **integration play** — being the one local company that bundles water intelligence + a
tracked health score + auto-populated service records + device monitoring into a single
relationship. That rides two documented tailwinds: insurance mandating leak devices (5–20%
discounts; water damage is ~24% of all homeowner claims, ~$13B/yr) and homeowners demanding
transparency, real-time updates, and financing (41% seek financing; offering it lifts close rates
~12%).

## Phase 9 — SWOT of the players to beat

**The Plumbing Experts (the scale leader)**
- _S:_ Largest fleet, GPS dispatch, 100k+ customers, financing, 48-yr brand. _W:_ Mediocre
  ratings (3.3–3.8★) — volume eroding quality perception. _O:_ Their unhappy customers are your
  funnel. _T:_ Marketing budget and dispatch density you can't match early.

**All Star Plumbing & Air (the quality+scale leader)**
- _S:_ 4.7★ across ~1,000 reviews, plumbing+HVAC, 24/7. _W:_ No visible water-intelligence layer.
  _O:_ Out-educate them on water. _T:_ Strong reputation = hard to dislodge head-on.

**Cleary Plumbing & Air (the modern operator)**
- _S:_ Membership ($15/mo), GreenSky financing, top-1% BuildZoom, no emergency upcharge. _W:_ No
  water-quality/education content. _O:_ Beat them on the intelligence layer they're missing. _T:_
  Closest to your model — most likely to copy you.

**National franchises (Ben Franklin, Roto-Rooter, bluefrog)**
- _S:_ Standardized programs, on-time guarantees, online booking, financing. _W:_ Thin local
  review depth; price-gouging complaints (the $14k repipe). _O:_ Be the trusted _local_ face.
  _T:_ Brand recognition and ad spend.

**Specialists (American Leak Detection, Atlantic Filter)**
- _S:_ Category dominance, deep expertise. _W:_ Narrow scope. _O:_ Partner or out-bundle. _T:_ If
  you enter water treatment, Atlantic Filter is the incumbent expert (since 1955).

## Recommended positioning

Become **the water-intelligence + communication company** for Palm Beach County:

1. **Lead with water science nobody else publishes** — an address-specific report (hardness +
   chloramine + PFAS status by utility), free, as the lead magnet.
2. **Win the communication complaint** — text updates, live arrival tracking, photo/video
   summaries, a customer portal. The #1 dissatisfaction driver, unguarded locally.
3. **Own the Plumbing Health Score** as the retention/standardization play — the clearest genuine
   white space.
4. **Wrap, don't build,** the device/records/predictive layers — install and monitor Moen/Phyn,
   auto-populate records from your own jobs, ride the insurance-discount tailwind.

Match competitors on financing and a membership plan (table stakes); differentiate on
intelligence, transparency, and water science.
